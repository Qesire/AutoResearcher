# Scientific Writing — Construct, Explain, Verify

**Activate** for a paper, abstract, introduction, methods, experimental-results section, manuscript review, LaTeX/typesetting review, scholarly figure/caption, reference audit or rebuttal. For open-ended mechanism discovery, use the existing research skills; for claims about novelty or publication literature, pair with [web-research-discovery](../web-research-discovery/SKILL.md) and inspect primary sources. Routine proofreading needs no broad literature search.

**Governing principles:** Follow the user's overarching work-and-expression instructions. Use [scientific manuscript review](../../../core/SCIENTIFIC_MANUSCRIPT_REVIEW.md) for the detailed review contract and [constructive scientific expression](../../../core/CONSTRUCTIVE_SCIENTIFIC_EXPRESSION.md) for prose. These references are loaded for the relevant part of a task, not copied into every answer.

1. **IDENTIFY THE SCIENTIFIC CLAIM.** Determine the research object, actual setting, gap, proposed contribution, nearest credible alternatives, primary evidence and intended venue. Trace a claim to a derivation, source, measurement or explicitly marked hypothesis. Preserve the distinction between a new mechanism, a new setting, and an integration of known components.
2. **BUILD THE ARGUMENT.** Organize abstract and introduction around a concrete problem, real gap, mechanism or idea, supported contribution and measured outcomes. If an application motivates the paper, specify who/what/where it serves and the conditions under which that application actually occurs. Make experiments answer the paper's central question, through a purposeful progression or parallel comparison.
3. **EXPLAIN MATHEMATICS.** At the first appearance of each symbol in a formula-heavy manuscript, provide an adjacent `where` explanation of the mathematical object, indexes, domain/shape and units where applicable. Expand acronyms on first use (check abstract and main text separately when the venue treats them separately). Keep equation symbols, subscripts, figures and code descriptions semantically consistent.
4. **INTERPRET RESULTS.** Connect each experiment's question, controlled comparison, outcome and inference. Discuss effect sizes, variation, counterexamples and valid limits; separate observed association from causal mechanism and local proxies from end-to-end outcomes. An unflattering result remains usable evidence. State uncertainty precisely beside the affected claim instead of front-loading defensive disclaimers.
5. **VERIFY THE PRESENTATION.** Review actual figures and rendered pages when accessible: labels, axes, units, visual hierarchy, color-blind accessibility, text sizes, legend semantics, subfigure tags, borders/grids, and caption agreement. Check notation, number–unit spaces, references, capitalization, punctuation, unnecessary hyphens and purposeful tense consistency against the target journal template.
6. **REVISE CONSTRUCTIVELY.** Prefer precise nouns, meaningful verbs, explicit relations and given-to-new sentence flow. Open with the supported finding or scientific construction. Replace ritualistic negations and defensive qualifications with the smallest concrete evidence boundary. Retain necessary negative findings, exclusions, counterexamples and honest limitations.
7. **DELIVER THE ACTUAL WORK.** For drafting, provide complete usable prose; for reviewing, prioritize claim/logic defects over format, identify the affected passage, explain why it matters and supply a localized fix. For source assets or venue rules that were not supplied/inspected, state only the particular verification still needed. Never invent experimental results, cited sources or visually verified properties.

**Modes:** `DRAFT` (argument → prose), `REVISE` (meaning-preserving edits), `REVIEW` (substantive + editorial findings), `FINAL_QA` (submission-specific checks). Apply the subset relevant to the user's task. A manuscript's scientific argument determines its structure; the checklist helps validate the result rather than dictate how every paragraph must look.
